# Durham, NC — Short-term rental legislative history

**Summary:** Durham has no dedicated city short-term-rental ordinance. Binding post-2008 STR-relevant law is primarily North Carolina’s rental-registration/permit preemption sequence (S.L. 2011-281 → 2016-122 → 2019-73 → 160D-1207 via 2019-111/2020-25), plus S.L. 2019-246 facilitator occupancy-tax duties. Local zoning still routes hosted paid lodging through the joint City-County UDO Bed and Breakfast use (pre-2008 UDO). Airbnb began collecting Durham County’s 6% occupancy tax on **2015-06-01**. No Airbnb–city data connection found.

## Main findings

1. **No dedicated city STR framework.** Official and secondary sources confirm Durham City/County have no stand-alone STR permit or registry. NCGS 160D-1207(c) bars general rental registration/renting-permission schemes. Hosted overnight lodging in residential districts is addressed under the joint UDO Bed and Breakfast limited use (§5.3.4.D: owner/manager on-site; minor special-use permit in many residential districts).

2. **State preemption is the primary post-2008 regulatory constraint.** Same statutory chain as Charlotte: S.L. 2011-281, 2016-122, 2019-73, and Chapter 160D recodification. *Schroeder v. City of Wilmington* (2022) confirmed the registration bar applies to STR schemes.

3. **Occupancy tax is county-level.** Durham County levies a 6% room occupancy tax (under-90-day stays, including Airbnb-style rentals). The City does not levy a separate municipal occupancy tax; local acts share a portion of county proceeds with the City.

4. **Airbnb tax collection:** **2015-06-01** — Airbnb’s May 18, 2015 announcement covered Durham County among the first four NC counties for occupancy-tax collection (with statewide sales tax).

5. **Airbnb data sharing:** **null** — City Portal launch listed Raleigh, not Durham; no Durham portal/API/compliance feed documented. NC remittances are described as lump-sum without host-level identification.
