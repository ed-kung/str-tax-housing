# Orlando, FL — Short-term rental legislative history

**Summary:** Orlando’s STR regime rests on a grandfathered **1991 Commercial Dwelling Unit / hotel-motel Land Development Code framework** that long barred whole-home short-term rentals in ordinary residential zones, preserved by Florida **Ch. 2011-119** and **Ch. 2014-71**. **Ordinance 2018-3** (passed 2018-02-12; effective **2018-07-01**) created the city’s modern hosted “owner-occupied home sharing” registration pathway while keeping unhosted whole-home rentals as Commercial Dwelling Units. **Ch. 2025-190 (SB 180)** freezes more restrictive local land-development rules through October 1, 2027. Airbnb began collecting Orange County’s 6% Tourist Development Tax for Orlando-area stays in **April 2016**; no City of Orlando–Airbnb compliance data connection was found.

## Main findings

1. **1991 LDC Commercial Dwelling Unit framework is the grandfathered primary land-use ban.** Ord. of 9-16-1991 (Doc. #25094 / #25103) defined CDUs (7–29 day stays) and hotel/motel (<7 days), limited to specified non-residential/tourist zoning—effectively a residential whole-home STR ban that Florida Politics (2018) described as a ~26-year prohibition. Complaint-driven enforcement continued into the Airbnb era.

2. **State preemption grandfathered Orlando’s restrictions.** Ch. 2011-119 (eff. 2011-06-02) and Ch. 2014-71 (eff. 2014-07-01) bar local prohibition or duration/frequency regulation of vacation rentals except pre-6/1/2011 ordinances—preserving Orlando’s CDU framework while requiring DBPR vacation-rental licensing for qualifying operators.

3. **Ordinance 2018-3 is the modern residential home-sharing framework.** Passed 2018-02-12; substantive rules effective 2018-07-01. Allows hosted, on-site home sharing citywide in residential districts with annual registration, residency proofs, ≤50% of unit, one booking / guest limits, duplex exception, listing of registration approval, and Class II civil penalties. Whole-home unhosted STRs remain CDUs in allowed zones only.

4. **Ch. 2025-190 (SB 180)** constrains more restrictive local land-development rules through 2027; Orange County has been directly affected (Vision 2050 challenge; multi-jurisdiction suit).

5. **Airbnb tax collection:** **2016-04-01** — Florida Politics (2016-04-15) reported Airbnb began collecting Orange County tourist/hotel taxes in April 2016 (county TDT covers City of Orlando; no separate city TOT). Exact day within April not specified; first-of-month used. State sales tax alone not recorded.

6. **Airbnb data sharing:** **null** — no City of Orlando–Airbnb City Portal or listing-level compliance connection found; enforcement relies on code enforcement / third-party listing monitoring and host-posted registration proof.
