# St. Petersburg, FL — Short-term rental legislative history

**Summary:** St. Petersburg’s Airbnb-era STR regime is a grandfathered **Chapter 16 Land Development Regulations transient-accommodation framework (Ord. No. 821-G, passed 2007-08-09; effective 2007-09-10)** limiting residential rentals under 30 days to three occurrences per rolling 365-day year unless hotel/motel zoning or a Resort Facilities Overlay applies. Florida **Ch. 2011-119** and **Ch. 2014-71** preserved that pre–June 1, 2011 rule while imposing DBPR vacation-rental licensing and blocking new local duration/frequency schemes. **Ch. 2025-190 (SB 180)** freezes more restrictive local land-development rules through October 1, 2027 (Pinellas is in covered disaster declarations). Airbnb began collecting Pinellas County Tourist Development Tax for St. Petersburg stays on bookings effective **2015-12-01**; no City–Airbnb compliance data connection was found.

## Main findings

1. **Ord. 821-G / Chapter 16 is the primary grandfathered framework.** Municode credits Ord. No. 821-G (adopted August 9, 2007) with enacting current Chapter 16 LDRs (effective September 10, 2007 per city Comp Plan / zoning materials). Transient accommodation uses are defined so that offerings for less-than-monthly occupancy more than three times in any consecutive 365-day period are treated as hotel/motel-type uses—effectively capping Airbnb-style stays under 30 days at three per year in ordinary residential districts. The City’s official Transient Accommodation handout (updated 2023) restates this rule and notes City Attorney concerns that adopting a new “short-term rental” use could jeopardize grandfathering under s. 509.032(7), F.S. Some news accounts cite ~2001 origins; the binding LDR codification is 2007.

2. **No city STR registry; enforcement is complaint-driven.** St. Petersburg has not enacted a dedicated STR registration ordinance. Hosts remain subject to Florida DBPR vacation-rental licensing (where applicable), City Business Tax Receipt rules, and code enforcement of the three-rental cap. A 2024 committee discussion of “super fines” and registry ideas was not recorded here as binding STR legislation. Pinellas County’s 2025 Certificate of Use ordinance applies only in unincorporated Pinellas.

3. **State preemption and LDR freeze.** Ch. 2011-119 (eff. 2011-06-02) and Ch. 2014-71 (eff. 2014-07-01) bar local prohibition or duration/frequency regulation of vacation rentals except pre-6/1/2011 ordinances—preserving St. Pete’s LDR. Ch. 2025-190 constrains more restrictive local land-development rules through 2027 for covered jurisdictions including Pinellas/St. Petersburg.

4. **Airbnb tax collection:** **2015-12-01** — Pinellas County Tax Collector lists Airbnb as collecting/remitting county Tourist Development Tax for bookings effective that date (covers City of St. Petersburg; no separate city TOT). State sales tax alone is not recorded.

5. **Airbnb data sharing:** **null** — no City of St. Petersburg listing-level compliance/enforcement data connection found; Visit Tampa Bay’s 2020 City Portal role was tourism insights for the broader market, not St. Pete regulatory access.
